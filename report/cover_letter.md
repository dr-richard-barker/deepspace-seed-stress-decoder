# Cover letter — npj Microgravity

**To:** The Editors, *npj Microgravity*
**Re:** Submission of an Article for consideration
**Title:** *A cell-type-resolved atlas of deep-space stress susceptibility in the dry and germinating Arabidopsis seed*
**Corresponding author:** Richard Barker (Purdue University; The Collaborative Science Environment, PBC) — admin@cosecloud.com

---

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript for publication as an Article in *npj Microgravity*.

Seeds are the most practical propagule for off-world agriculture, yet how the spectrum of deep-space
stressors impinges on the **dormant and germinating seed** is largely uncharacterized — spaceflight
experiments overwhelmingly profile later developmental stages, not seeds. Our study closes this gap. We
build two reusable, FAIR tools — a **DeepSpace Stress-Recognition System (DSRS)** that identifies which
space stressor a transcriptomic signature resembles, and a **Germinating-Seed AutoDecoder (GSAD)** that
projects bulk transcriptomes onto a single-cell dry/germinating-seed reference — and use them to assemble a
**cell-type-resolved susceptibility atlas** spanning microgravity, ionizing/galactic-cosmic radiation,
null and high magnetic fields, low-oxygen (hypoxia/anoxia/submergence), hypergravity, tropism, desiccation,
osmotic, ethylene/CO₂, temperature, and UV.

Two features should interest your readership. First, the framework is **validated by a strong positive
control**: restricted to the embryo lineage, it independently recovers textbook developmental
relationships from transcriptomes alone (protoderm→epidermis; hypophysis→radicle apical meristem;
vascular primordium→provasculature). Second, the atlas yields a clear, **falsifiable headline**: the
**radicle/root apical tip is the multi-stressor convergence hotspot**, and **early germination is the most
vulnerable developmental window** — pinpointing a priority cellular target for protecting seed performance
in deep space. We frame every prediction as concordance (not causation) with an explicit evidence tier and
a stated falsification experiment.

We believe this work fits *npj Microgravity* directly: it advances plant space biology and
bioregenerative life support, and it ships the analysis as openly licensed, documented, reproducible tools
and a versioned atlas that the community can apply to new datasets.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors approve submission. All
data are public (accessions listed in the manuscript); code and the atlas are openly available (GitHub;
Zenodo DOI on acceptance). The authors declare no competing interests.

Thank you for considering our submission.
